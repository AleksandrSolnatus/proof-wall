1. WHAT THE NEW PACKET CONTRIBUTES

The earlier common-pair detector automatically considered two-literal
conclusions and required each supporting clause to contain the whole pair.
Our more general S7 routine accepts an explicitly supplied candidate, denies
it, simplifies the whole source, and proves a consequence when the genuine
residual unit/binary portion is inconsistent.

Drago 003 automatically considers single signed literals L. It groups
normalized ternary clauses of the form L OR u OR v, denies L, and checks the
remaining binary rules for both x→NOT x and NOT x→x. Both paths carry original
clause references, and an independent verifier replays every edge.

Given the candidate [L], the S7 routine covers this pattern and additional
cases. Drago supplies automatic candidate enumeration and a direct unit
certificate. It is an engineered application of established 2-SAT reasoning.
The relevant primary background is the opposite-literal path criterion:
https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/classes/sp13/cse200-a/handouts/lec-06-0417.pdf

The symbol ∨ means inclusive OR; ¬ means NOT. A literal is a variable or its
negation. A unit conclusion fixes one variable's value.

2. DEMONSTRATION AND ORDINARY-RESOLUTION BRIDGE

The four original demonstration rules are:

  A ∨ ¬B ∨ C
  A ∨ ¬C ∨ ¬B
  A ∨ B ∨ D
  A ∨ ¬D ∨ B

Assuming A is false leaves B→C→¬B and ¬B→D→B. Either value of B contradicts
that assumption, so A is forced.

Three ordinary resolutions show the same result:

  Rules 1 and 2, resolving C, give A ∨ ¬B.
  Rules 3 and 4, resolving D, give A ∨ B.
  Those two clauses, resolving B, give A.

The original source has eight models, all with A true. Removing any one of
the four premises gives ten models, including a model with A false. Our
earlier pair detector already finds the two intermediate binary clauses;
the final resolution turns them into the unit.

The new adapter converts Drago's supplied paths into the ordinary-resolution
format. It does not discover candidates. For all 72 archived positive cases,
both the adapted proof and an independent S7 proof, given only the raw source
and candidate, pass the separate source-bound resolution verifier. Each route
produces 40 three-step and 32 seven-step proofs, totaling 344 resolution steps.
These are verified generated proofs, not claims of minimum proof length.

3. WHY THERE ARE EXACTLY 72 HITS

The exhaustive experiment examines every four-clause subset of 32 distinct
non-tautological ternary clauses on four variables: choose(32,4)=35,960.

Independent reconstruction from forbidden-assignment bitmasks confirms:

  35,960 formulas are satisfiable.
  35,888 have no forced literal.
      72 have exactly one forced literal.
       9 cases occur for each of the eight signed literals.
       0 semantic misses or false positives occur in this finite family.

Thus 72/35,960 describes the prevalence of forced literals in the corpus. It
does not mean the detector recognized only 72 out of many more forced cases.
The uploaded Counsel review had already established this finite agreement.
Our addition is the structural explanation below, plus independent replay.

Theorem for the narrow class. Let F contain at most four non-tautological
clauses, each normalizing to exactly three literals on distinct variables.
Each clause excludes 2^(n−3) of the 2^n assignments. Four clauses exclude at
most 2^(n−1), so at least half the assignments remain.

If F forces L, every surviving assignment lies in the L=true half. Both
halves have size 2^(n−1), so the models of F must be exactly the entire
L=true half. No clause may exclude an assignment in that allowed half.
Therefore every clause must contain L with the same sign. Denying L leaves
an inconsistent binary formula, which the shared-literal rule detects.

Consequences: for this at-most-four-clause ternary class, the rule is complete
for forced literals, for any n. With fewer than four such clauses, there is
not enough excluded-assignment capacity to force a literal. No formula in
this class is UNSAT. This is a narrow counting theorem, not a general SAT
algorithm. Having exactly half the assignments survive by itself does not
imply a forced literal; the additional containment in one literal's half
is essential.

For n=4 and a fixed signed L, the four forbidden sets are four disjoint edges
covering the eight vertices of the remaining three-variable cube. There are
nine perfect matchings of that cube, independently enumerated in the packet.
Eight signed choices of L give 8×9=72. This explains the observed count.

4. ONE FIFTH CLAUSE BREAKS THE GUARANTEE

The following focused control has seven models, all with A true:

  (1) A ∨ B ∨ C
  (2) A ∨ B ∨ ¬C
  (3) A ∨ ¬B ∨ D
  (4) A ∨ ¬D ∨ C
  (5) ¬B ∨ ¬C ∨ ¬D

Under NOT A, the first four rules force B=C=D=true. Their binary portion is
consistent. The fifth, still ternary, forbids that assignment. Both Drago's
restricted shared-literal detector and the immediate-residual S7 routine
miss A. This is not a counterexample to their soundness: they return no
certificate, not an incorrect conclusion.

A supplied four-step resolution proof is independently accepted:

  (1)+(2), resolve C:       A ∨ B
  (4)+(5), resolve C:       A ∨ ¬B ∨ ¬D
  previous+(3), resolve D:  A ∨ ¬B
  the two binary results:  A

This separates the existence of a short proof from the ability of the
selected recognition methods to find it. The explicit proof is recorded as
supplied algebra, not automated discovery by either restricted detector.

5. THE ORIGINAL REPLAY DEFECT AND THE SEPARATE REPAIR

The delivered search.py globbed EVIDENCE/*.json and treated any JSON list as
a CNF fixture. EVIDENCE/SERIES_CONTINUITY.json was added later and is a list
of metadata objects. Its entries are not clauses. The script reaches that
file after completing the 35,960-formula enumeration and fails while trying
to interpret metadata keys as integer literals.

The failed original execution, traceback, and completed deterministic
search outputs are retained. The failure does not invalidate the already
checked resolution and implication proofs. It does invalidate the claim
that the delivered unmodified search command replays successfully as-is.

The corrected copy distinguishes list-of-clause fixtures from metadata
lists and validates fixture literals. The replay harness changes only the
isolated copy of search.py. Its executed code hash is recorded separately.
The complete per-case rows, clause catalog, selected case, logical counters,
and fixture results are compared with retained evidence; timing and the
intended search-code hash change are explicitly excluded from equality.

The uploaded archive, detector, verifier, historical reports, manifests and
failed replay remain unchanged. No current Git or live Rulebench execution
is inferred from stored receipts. Local source bytes and stored receipt
bindings are independently checked.

All 47 package payload entries and 43 historical episode entries pass their
manifests. The 45 files listed by the saved Git receipt match local bytes,
SHA-256 values and Git blob IDs. Both saved Rulebench receipts pass all four
binding reconstructions and seven declared checks; model counts are checked
separately. This does not establish the current state of a remote branch.

The core entailment verifier rejects ten additional malformed controls both
normally and under Python -O. It validates the operative paths and source,
but does not validate the auxiliary kind, bucket-index, used-index or input
consistency annotations. Its returned consistency remains unchecked. The

6. THE FROZEN CONTINUATION PILOT

Question: does calling this detector once during an ongoing search save
enough remaining work to cover detection, proof checking and insertion?

Eight new constructed cases were fixed before evaluation. They contain
8 or 12 variables and 12–17 original clauses. Four include a six-clause
guarded contradictory pattern, with both signs of the conclusion; four
remove one guard clause. Half add an unrelated four-clause component.
They are targeted constructed cases, not a representative benchmark corpus.

The baseline is deterministic DPLL with unit propagation: smallest numbered
unassigned variable, false branch first, repeated full clause scans. A common
prefix stops after the first real decision and propagation to a fixed point.
Each arm receives the same copied assignment, full decision-frame stack,
source identity and propagation status. Backtracking preserves the continuing
search state; this is not a restart from the root.

The treatment invokes Drago once on the full immutable original CNF,
independently checks every emitted certificate against that source, then
inserts only verified global units. The full original clause prefix is
retained. Both arms continue the same solver and independently validate the
final satisfying assignment against every original clause.

There are 31 paired repetitions per case, with alternating arm order. Both
wall time and process CPU time include state copying, detection, verification,
unit processing/insertion, continuation and full-source witness validation.
Common-prefix work is recorded separately. Input loading, independent
post-run truth tables, receipt serialization, implementation and review are
outside those continuation timings. All named work counters remain separate;
no heterogeneous sum is advertised as CPU work.

The input cases, plan and runner were hashed before first evaluation. No
case, trigger, solver order or cap was changed after results were seen.
All 496 timed solver arms completed and agreed with independent exhaustive
oracles. All reported SAT witnesses satisfy the original source. One extra
corrupted-certificate control was rejected before any unit insertion or
continuation search. No-hit and losing cases are retained.

Logical search work reinforces the distinction:

  Two intact 8-variable cases: 273→200 search literal visits, saving 73 each.
  Their 12-variable variants: 510→572 visits, spending 62 more each.
  Four missing-guard cases: no certificate and no search-literal saving.

Detection, full-source certificate replay and insertion are additional work
in every treatment invocation. A reduced search counter alone does not mean
reduced total continuation cost. Here, the measured complete continuation
cost is higher even in the two cases with search savings.

This is evidence against enabling this exact trigger unconditionally for
these cases and this solver. It does not prove no detector integration can
ever help, or predict performance on a broader SAT population. The first
negative run is preserved; replay timings will naturally vary.

All eight cases are easy SAT continuations: neither arm encounters a conflict
or backtrack. Consequently this pilot measures intervention overhead and
limited search savings, not the value of avoiding a large failed subtree.
That limitation is part of the result, not an unperformed hard-case test.

8. PRESERVATION AND A PRECISE COMPRESSION OPPORTUNITY

A certified common-L bucket B has a stronger property than mere entailment:

  B = AND_i(L OR R_i) = L OR AND_i R_i = L,

because the residual conjunction is inconsistent. L also satisfies every
clause in that bucket. For a whole formula B AND U, an equivalent working
representation is therefore [L] AND U, keeping every unrelated constraint
U and the complete original source/proof as evidence.

This observation concerns a certified shared-literal bucket. It does not
authorize replacing an arbitrary full formula by a consequence, and it does
not automatically apply to S7 proofs assembled from different portions of
a larger candidate. The current pilot only inserts verified units; it does
not implement or benchmark bucket replacement.

The useful next question is consequently narrower than “does the scout work?”
It works as a recognizer on its stated class. The remaining cost question is
whether a justified trigger or a retained-source equivalent working view can
make its overhead worthwhile. This review stops after the one frozen pilot;
it does not tune a policy on these outcomes and present it as a fresh test.
