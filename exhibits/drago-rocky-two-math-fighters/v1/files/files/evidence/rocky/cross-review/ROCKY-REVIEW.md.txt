## Additional parity result

The six-variable, 16-clause CNF was grouped by its three-variable supports. Each four-clause group was exhaustively checked against all eight local assignments and recognized as exactly one stated XOR equation. XORing the four equations cancels every variable and yields RHS 1: a checked 0=1 contradiction, bound to all original clause groups rather than assumed from their appearance.

Separately, ordinary Davis-Putnam elimination on the ORIGINAL 16 CNF clauses generated a 31-derived-step resolution refutation, with 109 positive/negative parent-pair attempts. Every step was independently replayed for exact resolution and the last clause is empty. Full parents, pivots, clause lists and node IDs are in ROCKY-VERIFICATION.json. This is an established resolution procedure on a small fixed instance, not an invention, a minimum-length proof, a performance comparison to Drago, or a general efficient SAT result. No Drago engine was modified or credited with this new trace.

All 16 one-clause-deletion controls became satisfiable (two models each) and were rejected as complete XOR encodings by the recognizer. ## Opinion / next experiment

The review's main mathematical diagnosis is supported by the supplied examples: sound local consequences and shared proof bookkeeping do not make a restricted discovery process complete. The 15-to-4 change is a concrete proof-work saving on one diagnostic, not general runtime dominance. The parity obstruction is an inference-discovery boundary, not a soundness defect demonstrated by these records.

A useful next candidate is a bounded, source-bound parity recognizer with independently checked row-combination certificates and explicit failure on missing clauses. Alternatively a bounded general-resolution fallback can expose this fixed contradiction using the existing resolution proof language. These are different capabilities and should be compared with discovery costs, failed candidates, maximum intermediate width, and representation/translation work included. Neither reordering a disabled rule set nor a short certificate alone establishes fast general discovery.

Keep the current zero-step baseline, original proof histories and corrected ancestry example. Do not silently replace the existing programs or interpret UNKNOWN closure as SAT. No P/NP outcome, novelty, universal speedup or historical authorship claim follows.
