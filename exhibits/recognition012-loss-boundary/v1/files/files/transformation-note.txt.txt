AREPO 012 — RECOGNITION UNDER TRANSFORMATION
Mathematical note and invariant ladder
8 November 2026 | Exploratory mathematics | No metaphysical theorem asserted

ORIGINAL QUESTION
How far can a shape change and still be recognized as the same thing?

ANSWER IN ONE LINE
There is no single maximum transformation. The limit depends on the equivalence relation, the information allowed to survive, and the kind of recognition requested.

I. A LADDER OF RECOGNITION

LEVEL 1 — METRIC CONGRUENCE
  Preserve all distances. Translation, rotation, and reflection are allowed.
  A circle and its rotated copy are congruent; rescaling usually breaks congruence.

LEVEL 2 — GEOMETRIC SIMILARITY
  Preserve ratios of distances and angles up to uniform positive scale.
  Every nondegenerate circle satisfies circumference / diameter = pi.
  Radius R -> R/2 gives circumference C/2 and area A/4.
  Rotation, reflection, translation, and uniform scaling preserve similarity.

LEVEL 3 — TOPOLOGICAL TYPE
  Preserve a continuous one-to-one correspondence with a continuous inverse.
  Every simple closed plane curve is topologically equivalent to a circle.
  The curve can stretch dramatically and cease to be circular. A cut changes
  its topology; a self-intersection can also destroy the simple-curve property.

LEVEL 4 — ABSTRACT PARTS AND RELATIONSHIPS
  Preserve an explicitly chosen combinatorial structure: labels, adjacency,
  origin IDs, ordered boundary positions, or a documented cut graph.
  Two fragments need not form a circle to be recognized as parts of that circle.
  But merely seeing two similar arcs does not establish their original origin.

LEVEL 5 — TRACEABLE ORIGIN (PROVENANCE)
  Preserve evidence of the source and the operations performed on it.
  A reliable source record can establish which circle produced fragments even
  when no visual similarity or topological equivalence survives.
  However, a logged source is EXTERNAL information; it does not make the
  final geometry invertible, and source records require verification.

LEVEL 6 — INFORMATION LOSS
  If the final image and all retained metadata are identical for two different
  possible originals, no observer receiving only that information can uniquely
  distinguish which original was used. A source ID or stored original can
  supply the missing information, but that is additional input.

II. THE TEST FAMILY

  F_a(theta) = R [1 + a cos(3 theta)] (cos theta, sin theta),
  theta in [0, 2 pi), R > 0, a >= 0.

  a = 0:      perfect circle, similarity and topology both preserved.
  0 < a < 1:  positive radial distance for all theta, hence a simple closed
               star-shaped curve. Circularity is lost; topology is preserved.
  a = 1:      r(theta) = 0 at theta = pi/3, pi, 5pi/3. Three distinct
               positions meet at the origin. The parametrization is not
               injective: simple-closed-curve recognition fails.
  a > 1:      at least six distinct angles can map to the origin. More
               crossings appear. A known construction history can remain.

This is a LIMIT IN THIS ONE FAMILY, not a universal maximum of shape change.
The family has a supremum a=1 for preserving the simple-loop property, but
no largest permitted a<1. Arbitrarily severe stretching, even by a factor of
one trillion, still preserves similarity if the scaling is uniform and nonzero.

III. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE CIRCLE IS CUT?

Remove two short open angular neighborhoods centered at theta=0 and theta=pi.
The remaining SOURCE boundary consists of two separate labeled arcs. They are
no longer a single closed circle. Their embedded images can nevertheless touch
or overlap after extreme bending or collapse, so two abstract arcs does not
necessarily mean two geometrically disconnected visible components.

IV. LOSS OF INVERTIBILITY

Collapse each point toward the origin by a factor (1-c), 0 <= c <= 1.
For c<1, this scaling is invertible on the plane. At c=1, all positions
coincide at one point. The final dot has many possible origins; without an
independent record, unique reconstruction is impossible.

Mathematically, if f(A)=f(B) with A != B, then no function g receiving only
f(A) can always satisfy both g(f(A))=A and g(f(B))=B.

V. THREE DIFFERENT CLAIMS — DO NOT CONFUSE THEM

  (a) SAME FORM: equal up to the selected mathematical equivalence.
  (b) SAME SOURCE: a traceable history connects one object to another.
  (c) SAME BEING: an ontological or physical identity claim.

The geometric game can investigate (a), and with authentic records it can
support (b). It does not prove (c), human or machine consciousness, or that
all physical things are literally one entity.

VI. THE RECOGNITION GAME

Start with a unit circle. Uniformly rescale, rotate, bend until the simple
loop fails, cut it into two labeled arcs, separate them, then collapse them.
Toggle the provenance ledger separately. Watch when exact similarity,
topological shape, pointwise injectivity, and reconstructibility fail.

The testable question is not "Does everything remain the same?" It is:
"Which information do we require for this particular kind of recognition,
and at exactly which operation is that information lost?"

MATHEMATICAL REFERENCES/CONTEXT
Euclidean similarity, circle formulas, Jordan curves/homeomorphisms,
parametric curves, finite information and injectivity. These are established
mathematical concepts; the toy's chosen construction is an experiment.

THE WORKING STONE
Recognition survives only relative to the relations and records chosen to
survive. There is no absolute limit independent of what "same" means.
