<div class="eyebrow">MATH / ZERO-UP 001</div><h1>Turn the schedule inside out</h1><p>Begin at <strong>00:00</strong>. Add real machine obligations. Try not to pay more time than you must. During planning, let proposed operations cross; before execution, rebuild an entirely valid timetable.</p>
<section><h2>1. A tiny schedule that grows from zero</h2><p class="mini">Job A: Machine 1 for 3 minutes, then Machine 2 for 1. Job B: Machine 2 for 3, then Machine 1 for 1. All job sequences and machine times are original, not shortened.</p>

<section><h2>2. Why allowing a crossing matters</h2><p class="mini">The same machine must exchange the order of a 3-minute task A and a 1-minute task B. Move the slider between two valid schedules. Middle positions are a planning draft only.</p>

<section><h2>3. The actual 600-operation experiment</h2><p class="mini">30 original jobs, 20 original machines. Machines enter in descending order of required processing load. The scheduler rebuilds all active machine orders at each step; every selected checkpoint is feasible for that stage's requirements.</p><div class="line"><div><div class="mini">Machines activated</div><div class="big" id="stageNumber">0/20</div></div><div><div class="mini">Finish</div><div class="big" id="stageTime">0</div></div></div><input class="range" id="stages" type="range" min="0" max="20" step="1" value="0" aria-label="Number of machines activated"/><div class="plot" id="plot"></div><div class="labels"><span>0 machines</span><span>20 machines</span></div><p class="note" id="stageDesc">Start: zero work, zero time.</p><p class="mini">This is a recorded experiment, not a live optimization solver. A later recorded finish may be lower than an earlier one because each stage recomputes a fresh heuristic schedule; the true optimum cannot decrease when constraints are added.</p></section>
<section><h2>What this test taught</h2><p>The first strategy froze earlier machine orders, and the best completed result was 5,550. Letting the planner rebuild orders at each stage lowered the result to 2,429. Adding individual operations greedily gave 2,427. These are <strong>valid schedules, not proven optima</strong>—and older work has produced shorter ones.</p><p>The crossing in section 2 is the essential distinction: <strong>an invalid draft is allowed; an invalid final schedule is not.</strong> We never remove jobs, change their duration, or alter their required routes in the final output.</p></section>
