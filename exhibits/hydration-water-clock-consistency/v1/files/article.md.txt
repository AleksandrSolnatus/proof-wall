# When the water moves but the clock doesn't

A water-transfer equation can be correct while its numerical implementation tells an inconsistent story. In this experiment, a requested step could move simulated water without changing the stored clock. Another could report a transfer too small to change the stored water. A third could reach a boundary in a computed time of zero while moving a nonzero amount.

The useful result was learning when the implementation must refuse to advance. The final artifact is a small, explicitly uncalibrated model with its corrections and synthetic checks attached.

## The original question

Could a contained hydration model move water between two finite compartments, preserve its water account, stop at the first declared boundary, and resume from a saved state without inventing the next biological event?

The compartments are called seed and bath. Those names describe the intended question; the supplied test values are artificial reservoirs, not measured seed coefficients. No germination or growth result is claimed.

## The mathematical starting point

The chosen hypothesis gives each compartment a positive, constant storage capacity. Its water potential changes linearly with its water mass. A specified nonnegative conductance makes water move down the potential difference. The bath loses what the seed gains, and the transfer may reverse when the potential difference reverses.

Let DeltaPsi0 be the initial bath-minus-seed potential difference, a = 1/C_seed + 1/C_bath, and k = L*a. Within the declared domain, the signed transfer after duration t is:

```
x(t) = (DeltaPsi0/a) * (1 - exp(-k*t))
```

The implementation evaluates the last factor with -expm1(-k*t), avoiding the direct subtraction of nearly equal numbers for small arguments. This is the closed-form solution of the selected linear hypothesis, subject to floating-point arithmetic. It is not an exact description of living seed physiology.

State stores total water and seed water; bath water is their difference. A step records the new state, advanced time, unadvanced time, and signed transfer. When the first declared seed-domain limit, empty bath, or empty seed is reached, the model returns the boundary time and leaves the remainder unadvanced. A boundary state refuses another step under the same hypothesis.

## Three failures worth keeping

### Water moved while the stored clock stood still

The independent review found that a stored elapsed time of 1e20 plus a requested increment of 1 could round back to the same value. A calculation could therefore move water while its stored time did not advance.

The correction checks that positive requested time produces a positive stored increment and that the increment agrees with the requested duration within a declared relative tolerance of 1e-12. Exhausted clock precision causes refusal. The final tests include this case for transfer, zero-conductance, equilibrium, and boundary configurations.

### A reported transfer did not change the stored water

Read-only Counsel found a tiny analytic transfer that could be reported even though rounding left the stored water unchanged. Improving the exponential calculation alone did not solve the endpoint representation problem.

The correction refuses a nonzero transport step when either represented water stock fails to change. Its transfer receipt reports the stored seed-water endpoint difference. Ordinary representable transfers still have floating-point rounding; the receipt does not claim arbitrary precision.

### A nonzero boundary crossing acquired a zero time

A later edge case exposed underflow in the boundary fraction or event time: nonzero water could move to a boundary while the computed event time became zero.

The correction added explicit refusals for those underflows and a sixteenth regression test. Genuine zero-time, zero-transfer outward boundary events remain supported. The model does not forbid every zero-time event merely to remove the troublesome case.

The work also preserves a corrected test expectation: an initially rejected extreme-duration request actually had a valid finite boundary event. That expectation was replaced with a genuinely overflowing rate-duration case; no implementation safeguard was weakened to satisfy the test.

## What was already checked

The October 9, 2026 record reports 16 passing synthetic tests after the final correction, an independent final implementation review, and a final read-only Counsel inspection and 16-test pass. The tests cover the analytic solution, the stored water account, reverse flow, equilibrium, boundary events, remaining time, precision refusals, endpoint receipts, and snapshot continuation and rejection.

## Where the support stops

- Constant conductance, linear storage, and persistent contact are explicit, uncalibrated assumptions. Their organism-level error is not bounded.
- No measured lotus parameter set, metabolism, leakage, evaporation, tissue expansion, viability diagnosis, rupture, or germination is implemented.
- The stored total is retained by the state representation; separate floating-point endpoint sums and transfers still require numerical care.
- Restoring the same valid snapshot preserves the tested continuation. Splitting durations agrees within roundoff; it is not generally a bitwise identity.
- Sixteen tests and scoped independent review support the retained numerical artifact. They do not establish that all floating-point inputs or biological processes are handled correctly.

## The unfinished question

What constitutive hypothesis and measurements would justify moving beyond these artificial compartments? Water uptake alone cannot identify both conductance and storage. A biological continuation needs its own evidence; a numerical boundary does not supply it.

The portable lesson is smaller: a simulation receipt should describe a change the stored state can actually represent. When it cannot, a clear refusal is useful information.
