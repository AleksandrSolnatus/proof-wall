Eight cells, one disappearing view

verified finite result

An isolated geometry/state encounter with recorded desktop browser checks; neither 4D physics nor a learning-effectiveness study.

The question

Can the same eight tesseract boundary cells remain identifiable as a helpful display becomes a collapsed projection?

The method

Exact source incidence, seven retained hinges, full-cell teaching markers, declared interpolation to XYZ projection and versioned study replay.

The finding

The geometry preserves cell rigidity and retained hinge matches; strict projection keeps identities even when visible geometry coincides.

Research text · rendered from the supplied Markdown

Eight cells, one disappearing view

Two objects can occupy the same visible place without being the same object. A projection can lose a distinction that the source still retains.

Fold to Collapse is an isolated study of that boundary. Its source is the boundary of the tesseract [-1,1]^4: 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces and eight cubic cells, with stable X-/X+, Y-/Y+, Z-/Z+ and W-/W+ identities.

Fold first, then remove the scaffold

The articulated net has seven retained hinges. Each cell remains rigid while the net folds. Cut boundaries use separate cell instances that refer to immutable source identities; at closure the source incidences return.

The initial display uses full-size cube markers centered on projected cell centers. These are explicitly nonliteral presence markers. They help a visitor follow the cells but do not pretend to show their literal projected shape.

After folding, a declared teaching interpolation turns the markers into the actual projection. Memory outlines and identity aids then fade. This interpolation is display deformation, not a new physical law.

The same picture, different places

The final observation is P0(x,y,z,w) = (x,y,z). It drops W.

The opposite W- and W+ cells become coincident cubes in this observation. Their source centroids are still two units apart in four dimensions. The other six cubic cells lose their W extent and appear as square sheets. The model preserves their IDs even where screen strokes have zero length or overlap.

Camera orbit and zoom are downstream views. They do not alter source topology or the four-dimensional construction. A saved study retains the versioned source definition, phase, selection, teaching recipe and camera; invalid or future definitions refuse before replacing current state.

What supports the result

The September 24 review records ten geometry/state tests and an independent 4,001-state sweep. Its reported maximum distance error was about 1.11e-15, with retained hinge error zero and exact sampled replay. Desktop Chromium and WebKit checks covered opening, saved-study replay, invalid-file preservation, controls and narrow layouts. These are numerical and software observations, not exact floating-point identities for all possible executions.

The November 10 source recovery separately replayed the ten existing core/state tests. That replay did not rerun the original browsers or establish a new physical-phone result. This publication reused the evidence rather than repeating the science.

What remains open

A mathematically truthful view is not automatically an effective lesson. No learning benefit was measured. The sidebar remains available, so this is not a blinded learning trial. Projection overlap is not evidence of collision, and the teaching display is not a general four-dimensional world engine.

Source and replay tests are provided as inert downloads. The two test files were originally core.test.cjs and state.test.cjs; their public .js copies retain the same bytes and relative source dependencies. The original interactive HTML is not activated by the archive.

Limits & open questions

  • Full-size cube markers and their transition are teaching displays, not literal 3D projections or physical deformations.
  • Projection coincidence is not source identity or collision.
  • No demonstrated teaching benefit, general hidden-W inference or native Arepo integration.
  • Desktop WebKit and narrow touch emulation do not establish physical iPhone loading or pinch behavior.

Still open

  • Which representation helps a visitor follow a cell after its visual separation and labels disappear?
