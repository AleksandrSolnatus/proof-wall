## Mathematical interpretation — provisional
Use Hebrew roots and productive word-building as an **analogy**, not an assertion about Hebrew or a mathematical theorem:

1. Agree on the smallest precisely defined primitives or shared facts: the “roots”.
2. Specify which transformations are licensed: prefixes, patterns, relations, and combinations as analogues of valid inference rules.
3. Derive increasingly expressive statements *with* the fixed problem by testing what each proposed rule actually preserves or entails.
4. Attend to what becomes forced and to what remains unresolved; do not require the problem to yield an answer.
5. Admit a new rule or remembered fact only if its meaning, soundness, verification cost, and reuse conditions survive scrutiny.

Possible notation: P=fixed problem, R=agreed primitives, G_n=currently accepted sound grammar, K_n=certified derived knowledge. "Listen" is an operational metaphor for *deriving entailed consequences*, not granting agency to a formula.

## Untested research question
Does a small, compositional grammar built from agreed problem primitives provide *transferable* savings in downstream search compared with (i) a frozen general-purpose grammar and (ii) direct search, **after charging grammar-building, translation, proof checking, communication, and memory**?

A future bounded test should predeclare how the grammar may grow; compare with identical-cost sham additions; test on held-out variable-renamed/reordered and genuinely new instances; log any soundness failure, ambiguity, or regression. It should also distinguish *syntactic* shared rules from *semantic* insight about a problem class. An instance-specific grammar can encode the answer, so compression/design cost and held-out transfer matter.
